**ARC (Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus) doesn’t have “explicit rules” written down — instead, it’s built around recurring *types of transformations* that humans intuitively recognize.** To solve ARC tasks, an agent must discover these hidden rules from examples.  

Here are the **basic categories of ARC rules** that cover most tasks:

---

## 🔑 Core Rule Families in ARC

| Rule Family | Examples | What It Tests |
|-------------|----------|---------------|
| **Color Mapping** | Replace red with blue, swap colors | Symbolic substitution, invariance |
| **Object Detection & Copying** | Identify shapes, copy them elsewhere | Segmentation, object permanence |
| **Symmetry & Reflection** | Mirror across vertical/horizontal axis | Spatial reasoning |
| **Rotation & Translation** | Rotate objects 90°, shift them | Geometric transformations |
| **Counting & Arithmetic** | Add/remove objects based on count | Numerical abstraction |
| **Connectivity & Flood Fill** | Fill enclosed areas, expand regions | Topological reasoning |
| **Pattern Extension** | Continue stripes, grids, or sequences | Inductive generalization |
| **Conditional Rules** | “If object is red, make it square” | Rule composition, branching logic |
| **Enclosure & Inside/Outside** | Paint inside loops, borders | Topological predicates |
| **Every-Nth Rule** | Paint every other row/column | Modular arithmetic, spatial predicates |
| **Object Correspondence** | Match input/output objects by size/position | Relational reasoning |
| **Global Constraints** | Preserve palette, object count, or symmetry | Conservation laws |

---

## 🧠 Why These Rules Matter
- **ARC tasks are designed to test generalization.** You can’t memorize outputs; you must infer rules.  
- **Humans solve ~80% of ARC tasks** because we naturally use these abstractions.  
- **AI systems struggle (~31% solved)** because they often lack symbolic reasoning.
